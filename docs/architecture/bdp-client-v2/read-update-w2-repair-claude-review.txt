## Findings

### 1 — LOW — `order-and-partial-commit`'s new disclosure says its shape controls live on the twin, but two of its assertions no longer have a counterpart there

**Location** `packages/conformance/test-support/read-update-coverage-v1.json:3913`; scenarios at `packages/conformance/matrices/read-update-v1.json:37413` vs `:40299`.

The appended reason reads "…its shape sensitivity controls live on member-problem-shape, which now additionally observes named Link and alias-result branches." That was exactly true at `0b34fe7`, where the two scenarios were byte-identical modulo `id`. It is no longer. Comparing the two `member-shapes` actions assertion-by-assertion: 27 ids in the order row, all present on the twin, **two with different content**:

| id | order row | member-problem-shape |
|---|---|---|
| `indices` | `equals: [[0],[1],[2],[3]]` | `equals: [[0],[1],[2],[3],[4],[5]]` |
| `no-extra-result` | `/body/results/4` `exists: false` | `/body/results/6` `exists: false` |

All twelve W2 controls dispatch on `currentScenario`, and `memberControl` is gated to `"read-update.sequence.member-problem-shape"` (`read-update-matrix.test.ts:359`). So the order row's four-entry `indices` tuple and its `/body/results/4` cardinality bound — the two assertions that would catch a fabricated fifth entry or a reordered result array on *its* four-member request — now have no sensitivity witness anywhere in the manifest, and the reason does not say so. The `json-array-tuples` + `ordered: true` mechanism is still proven discriminating by the `index` and `swap` controls on the twin; only this row's specific instances are unwitnessed.

**Impact** Disclosure precision on a row that is already `wholeContract: false` / "Incomplete", so no false completeness claim. A reviewer taking the reason at face value would believe every one of its 81 assertions is covered by the twin's controls; 79 are.

**Fix** Qualify the reason — "…except its four-entry `indices` tuple and its `/body/results/4` cardinality bound, which the six-member twin no longer mirrors" — or dispatch one extra-entry injection (`results.push(structuredClone(results[3]))`) at `order-and-partial-commit`, which closes both in one control.

### 2 — LOW — the raw-carrier control table now contains a case that must pass, under a title that says "detects … control"

**Location** `apps/bdptest/src/read-update-matrix.test.ts:1064` (`it.each([… "base64"])`), `:1087` (`expect(row?.state).toBe(control === "base64" ? "pass" : "fail")`), `:1092-1094` (`expect(failures).toEqual(control === "base64" ? [] : …)`).

The `base64` case is an encoding-equivalence check, not a counterfactual: it re-encodes `count-33` to base64, expects the row to **pass**, and expects an empty failure set. Its content is right and genuinely valuable — the authored value becomes 7,872 chars while the wire stays 5,903 octets, so the pre-repair `Buffer.byteLength(value)` comparison would have failed it, making it a real regression guard for finding 5's fix. But it sits in a parameterised list whose title is now false for one of its four rows, and a reader counting "W2 raw carrier controls" in the suite gets four where three are counterfactuals.

**Impact** Legibility of the control inventory only. No assertion is wrong.

**Fix** Lift it into its own `it("accepts an equivalent base64 carrier body with identical payload octets, refusal and corrected-key flow", …)`, leaving `it.each(["wrong-status","wrong-code","below-count-boundary"])` as a pure control table.

### 3 — LOW — "only the explicit body-size raw request consumes the larger 131072 harness budget" is asserted for the authored and client lanes but not for the fixture-seeding lane

**Location** claim at `docs/design/read-update-executable-progress.md` ("Sequence council repair", 4th paragraph) and `read-update-w2-repair-report.md:10`; guards at `apps/bdptest/src/read-update-matrix.test.ts:657-677`; uncovered writer at `:301-307`.

Its observations land in the separate `setupObservations` array and carry no `bodyOctets` field at all, so loop (c) structurally cannot reach them. In practice the largest seed body is 141 bytes and seeding only runs for the `read.*` rows in `readSeedScenarios`, so there is no reachable failure today — this is a gap between a stated universal and what is enforced, not a live risk.

**Fix** Either one more bound in the same block — `for (const entry of [...seed.beads, ...seed.links]) expect(Buffer.byteLength(JSON.stringify(entry))).toBeLessThanOrEqual(bodyLimit)` — or scope the sentence to "every authored scenario body".

- **No seeding or isolation side effects from the two new fixture bindings.** Seeding is driven by `fixture.readSeed` + `readSeedScenarios` (`installed-read-fixture.ts:61-80`), not `bindings`, and neither sequence row is in `readSeedScenarios` — so `links/w2-shape` and `alias/w2-shape` are created by the sequence under test, not pre-planted. The bindings add only route-allowlist entries (`read-update-matrix.test.ts:131-170`), which is precisely why `exactConfigurationDigest` moved; the "unused fixture binding" admission control is untouched and both new bindings are referenced. Every scenario gets its own child process and its own data directory (`:276-322`), so nothing leaks to the `alias.*` rows.
- **The client lane legitimately reaches the new Link identity.** `read-update-exact-client.ts:71-76` would refuse `links/w2-shape` as a captured read unless it is an installed fixed route; `edge-read` is a fixed `targetBinding` GET, so `clientReadRoutes` admits exactly `GET ${scope}links/w2-shape`. No wildcard, no relaxation of the canonical-Bead gate for captured identities.
- **The inventory bindings are mechanically exact.** I recomputed each planned sequence row's binding list against its own scenario's assertion ids in document order: member-problem-shape 101/101, carrier-rejection 1479/1479, order-and-partial-commit 81/81, local-bindings 177/177 — all EQUAL, so `cumulative-artifact.test.ts`'s one-row binding mechanism still constrains the new assertions rather than being bypassed.
